## 5. RFC-Specific IP Considerations

### 5.1 Normative vs. Informative Content

RFCs contain both normative (MUST/REQUIRED) and informative (MAY/OPTIONAL) content.

**Patent protection applies to:**
- ✅ Normative requirements (essential patents)
- ✅ Informative mechanisms (may be patented but not essential)
- ✅ Examples and illustrations (may embody patented mechanisms)
- ✅ Architectural patterns described in RFCs

### 5.2 RFC Dependencies

Some RFCs depend on or reference other RFCs.

**Patent licensing must cover all implemented RFCs and their dependencies.**

### 5.3 Conformance Levels

RFCs define conformance levels (L1, L2, L3, L4).

**Patent licensing requirements vary by conformance level:**
- **L1 (Persistence)** - Requires licenses to L1-essential patents
- **L2 (Deterministic Governance)** - Requires licenses to L1+L2-essential patents
- **L3 (Cross-Model Continuity)** - Requires licenses to L1+L2+L3-essential patents
- **L4 (Federation)** - Requires licenses to L1+L2+L3+L4-essential patents

**Higher conformance levels require broader patent licenses.**
